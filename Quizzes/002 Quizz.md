Here is the same question and answer entirely in English:

---

**Question:**  
If the seller is to pay the freight costs of delivering merchandise, the delivery terms are stated as:

a. FOB seller  
b. FOB destination  
c. FOB shipping point  
d. FOB n/30  

**Answer:**  
**b. FOB destination**

---

**Explanation:**  
- **FOB destination** means the seller retains ownership and responsibility for the goods until they reach the buyer's location. The seller pays the freight costs.  
- **FOB shipping point** means the buyer pays the freight costs and takes ownership once the goods leave the seller's location.  

*(Note: "FOB seller" is not a standard term, and "FOB n/30" refers to credit terms, not shipping terms.)*

**Question:**  
If the buyer is to pay the freight costs of delivering merchandise, delivery terms are stated as:

a. FOB destination  
b. FOB n/30  
c. FOB shipping point  
d. FOB buyer  

**Answer:**  
**c. FOB shipping point**

---

**Explanation:**  
- **FOB shipping point** means the buyer takes ownership of the goods as soon as they leave the seller's location, and the buyer is responsible for paying the freight costs.  
- **FOB destination** would mean the seller pays the freight costs (opposite of what the question asks).  
- **FOB n/30** refers to credit/payment terms, not shipping terms.  
- **FOB buyer** is not a standard shipping term.

**Question:**  
If title to merchandise purchases passes to the buyer when the goods are shipped from the seller, the terms are:

a. consigned  
b. n/30  
c. FOB shipping point  
d. FOB destination  

**Answer:**  
**c. FOB shipping point**

---

**Explanation:**  
- **FOB shipping point** means the title (ownership) of the goods transfers to the buyer as soon as the goods are shipped (leave the seller's location). The buyer also typically pays the freight costs.  
- **FOB destination** means the title transfers only when the goods arrive at the buyer's location (seller pays freight).  
- **Consigned** refers to goods sent on consignment, where title remains with the consignor until sold.  
- **n/30** refers to credit/payment terms (net 30 days), not shipping or title transfer.

**Question:**  
The balance sheet should be prepared:

a. before the income statement and the statement of stockholders' equity  
b. after the income statement and the statement of stockholders' equity  
c. before the income statement and after the statement of stockholders' equity  
d. after the income statement and before the statement of stockholders' equity  

**Answer:**  
**b. after the income statement and the statement of stockholders' equity**

---

**Explanation:**  
The balance sheet is prepared **after** the income statement and the statement of stockholders' equity because:

- The **income statement** must be completed first to determine net income (or net loss) for the period.  
- The **statement of stockholders' equity** uses that net income figure to calculate the ending retained earnings balance.  
- The **balance sheet** then uses that ending retained earnings figure as part of its stockholders' equity section.

**Correct order of preparation:**
1. Income Statement  
2. Statement of Stockholders' Equity  
3. Balance Sheet

**Question:**  
Which document authorizes the purchase of inventory from an approved vendor?

a. receiving report  
b. purchase order  
c. petty cash voucher  
d. vendor's invoice  

**Answer:**  
**b. purchase order**

---

**Explanation:**  
- A **purchase order** is issued by the buyer to the vendor and formally authorizes the purchase of inventory or other goods. It includes details such as quantities, prices, and delivery terms.  
- A **receiving report** documents the receipt of goods and their condition.  
- A **petty cash voucher** is used for small cash payments.  
- A **vendor's invoice** is the bill sent by the seller to the buyer after the goods have been ordered and delivered.

**Question:**  
Taking a physical count of inventory:

a. has no internal control relevance  
b. is not necessary when a periodic inventory system is used  
c. should be done near year-end  
d. is not necessary when a perpetual inventory system is used  

**Answer:**  
**c. should be done near year-end**

---

**Explanation:**  
- A physical count of inventory is **necessary** under **both** the periodic and perpetual inventory systems to verify accuracy, detect theft, spoilage, or recording errors.  
- It **does** have internal control relevance because it helps safeguard assets and ensure the accuracy of financial records.  
- While it can be done at various times, it is **typically performed near year-end** to ensure the inventory balance reported on the year-end balance sheet and cost of goods sold on the income statement are accurate for financial reporting purposes.

**Question:**  
All of the following are documents used for inventory control except a:

a. vendor's invoice  
b. receiving report  
c. petty cash voucher  
d. purchase order  

**Answer:**  
**c. petty cash voucher**

---

**Explanation:**  
- **Vendor's invoice**, **receiving report**, and **purchase order** are all key documents used in the inventory control process (often part of the three-way matching system to verify orders, receipts, and billing).  
- A **petty cash voucher**, on the other hand, is used to document small cash expenditures (like office supplies or minor expenses) and has **no direct role** in inventory control.

**Question:**  
Ending inventory is made up of the oldest purchases when a company uses the:

a. weighted average cost method  
b. last-in, first-out method  
c. retail method  
d. first-in, first-out method  

**Answer:**  
**b. last-in, first-out (LIFO) method**

---

**Explanation:**  
- Under the **LIFO (last-in, first-out)** method, the most recently purchased (newest) goods are assumed to be sold first. This means the goods remaining in ending inventory are the **oldest** purchases.  
- Under **FIFO (first-in, first-out)**, the oldest goods are sold first, so ending inventory consists of the newest purchases.  
- The **weighted average cost** method averages the cost of all goods available for sale, so ending inventory is not made up of specifically oldest or newest layers.  
- The **retail method** is an estimation technique, not a cost flow assumption.

- The **weighted average cost** method averages all costs together rather than following a specific order.  
- **First-in, last-out** is not a standard inventory costing method.

**Question:**  
If inventory is being valued at cost and the price level is steadily rising, the method of costing that will yield the highest net income is:

a. weighted average cost  
b. LIFO  
c. FIFO  
d. periodic  

**Answer:**  
**c. FIFO**

---

**Explanation:**  
During periods of **rising prices** (inflation):

- **FIFO (first-in, first-out)** assumes that the oldest (and therefore **cheapest**) goods are sold first. This results in a **lower cost of goods sold** and, consequently, **higher net income**.
- **LIFO (last-in, first-out)** assumes the newest (and most expensive) goods are sold first, leading to a **higher cost of goods sold** and **lower net income**.
- **Weighted average cost** smooths out price changes, resulting in a net income figure somewhere between FIFO and LIFO.
- **Periodic** is not a costing method; it's an inventory system (as opposed to perpetual).

So, in a rising price environment, **FIFO produces the highest net income**.

**Question:**  
The inventory cost flow method that reports the most current prices in ending inventory is:

a. weighted average cost  
b. LIFO  
c. FIFO  
d. specific identification  

**Answer:**  
**c. FIFO**

---

**Explanation:**  
- Under **FIFO (first-in, first-out)** , the oldest costs are assigned to cost of goods sold, and the **newest (most current) costs** remain in ending inventory. This means ending inventory reflects the most recent purchase prices.
- Under **LIFO (last-in, first-out)** , the newest costs go to cost of goods sold, so ending inventory consists of the oldest costs.
- **Weighted average cost** blends all costs together, so ending inventory reflects an average rather than the most current prices.
- **Specific identification** tracks the actual cost of each specific item, so it may or may not reflect current prices depending on which items are on hand.
